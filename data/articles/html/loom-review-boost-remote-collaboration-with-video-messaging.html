Choosing Loom: Comparisons, Buying Guide, and FAQs

How Loom Stacks Up Against the Competition

Loom’s biggest selling point is its freemium entry tier paired with an intuitive tutorial system. Competing async‑video platforms such as Vidyard, CloudApp, and Soapbox often require a paid subscription before users can record more than a handful of minutes, which raises the barrier for small or newly‑formed teams. While those services may offer deeper analytics or branded player options, Loom delivers a clean, no‑frills experience that lets anyone start recording within seconds.

Where Loom lags is its API reliability. Some users report occasional non‑responses when trying to integrate recordings with custom workflows. In contrast, higher‑priced alternatives tend to provide more robust developer support and guaranteed uptime. If your organization depends heavily on automated pipelines, you may need to weigh that risk against Loom’s lower cost.

Buying Guide: When to Stick with the Free Plan and When to Upgrade

Before committing to a paid tier, consider these three criteria:

  1. Team size and video volume – The free plan includes up to 25 videos per month and 5 GB of storage. Teams that regularly produce longer walkthroughs or need unlimited archives should look at the Starter or Business plans.
  2. Integration needs – If you rely on Slack, Jira, or Notion integrations, the free tier covers basic linking, but advanced automation (e.g., auto‑post to Confluence or webhook triggers) is only available on paid tiers.
  3. Support expectations – Paid plans unlock priority email support and a dedicated success manager, which can be crucial when the API hiccups occur.

For most startups and remote‑first squads, starting with the freemium version provides enough runway to test the async workflow. Upgrade only after you’ve confirmed that Loom’s core features solve your communication bottlenecks.

Alternatives Worth a Look

  • Vidyard – Strong analytics and enterprise‑grade security, but the free tier is extremely limited.
  • CloudApp – Combines screenshots, GIFs, and video; good for mixed media but the UI feels cluttered for pure video messaging.
  • Soapbox by Wistia – Excellent for marketing‑oriented videos with customizable CTAs; not ideal for internal quick updates.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Is Loom free? – Yes. A freemium tier is available with basic recording limits and 5 GB of storage.

  • How does the async workflow actually work? – Record a video, copy the generated link, and paste it into any communication channel (Slack, email, project board). Recipients watch at their convenience and can leave time‑stamped comments directly on the video.
  • Do paid plans include higher video limits? – Yes. The Starter plan raises the monthly video cap to 200 and adds 25 GB of storage; the Business plan removes caps entirely.
